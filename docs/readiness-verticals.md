Governance was one. It was not the first one that stops you.

Checked 25 October 2026. Legal and tax figures are from secondary guides and are
marked for confirmation. Nothing here is legal or tax advice.

## 1. Legal entity and getting paid

**The hardest blocker, and the least discussed.**

You have no company. No registration, no business bank account, no way to send
an invoice to a client in London and receive pounds. You cannot accept money for
this today, whatever the plan says.

### What has to exist

| Item | Note |
|---|---|
| A registered business | See below |
| A business bank account | Separate from personal, in the business name |
| A way to receive foreign currency | Most clients pay in USD or GBP |
| An invoice that is legally valid in Malaysia | See the e-invoice trap |
| A tax position | Which income, taxed how, and when |

### Sole proprietorship or Sdn Bhd

**For confirmation with SSM and a licensed accountant.** Figures from secondary
guides, 2026.

| | Sole proprietorship | Sdn Bhd |
|---|---|---|
| Who may register | Malaysian citizens and permanent residents | Anyone, including full foreign ownership |
| Cost | About RM 30 a year through EzBiz | About RM 1,010 to incorporate, plus a licensed company secretary within 30 days |
| Liability | **Unlimited and personal** | Limited to paid-up shares |
| Tax | Profit taxed as your personal income | Corporate rates, reported as 17 percent on the first RM 600,000 and 24 percent above |

**The liability line is the one that matters here.** This business publishes
living people's faces, private letters and recreated voices. A family dispute
that turns legal reaches a sole proprietor's personal assets without a barrier.
Against that, a company costs about a thousand ringgit and a secretary.

**Recommendation:** start as a sole proprietorship only if the first commission
is a friendly one you control. Incorporate before any client you did not already
know. **Ask a licensed accountant, not me, and not an AI.**

**Registration timing.** One guide states that anyone carrying on a trade for
gain in Malaysia, a solo freelancer included, must register with SSM within
**30 days** of beginning to earn. Confirm with SSM.

### The e-invoice trap

**This one is specific, and it applies to you from your first sale.**

Malaysia's e-invoicing regime, MyInvois, exempts small businesses below a
turnover threshold, which sources say rose to **RM 3 million** around
1 September 2026.

That exemption will not save you, because of a separate rule:

> **Any single invoice or receipt exceeding RM 10,000 must be issued as a full
> e-invoice through MyInvois, regardless of turnover.**

Your Chronicle is priced at **RM 14,000 to 22,000**. Every commission you ever
sell crosses that line.

So you are inside MyInvois from invoice number one, as a business with no
employees and no revenue yet. Nobody warns a new business about this, because
most new businesses do not issue five-figure invoices on day one.

**Sources conflict** on the threshold date and on the enforcement relaxation
windows. **Confirm with LHDN's own current guideline, or a licensed tax agent,
before you invoice anybody.**

### Receiving foreign money

Your plan targets the diaspora, paying in USD and GBP. That means a route for
receiving foreign currency, the exchange cost on each payment, and the tax
treatment of foreign-sourced income. None of it is set up, and none of it was
covered by the sources checked here. **Ask an accountant.**

## 2. The commercial contract

Governance drafted the **consent** paperwork. That is permission from the people
featured. It is not an agreement with the person paying you.

Still missing, entirely:

- Scope. What a Chronicle includes, and what it does not.
- What the family must supply, and by when.
- Price, payment stages, and what happens when they pay late.
- How many rounds of changes are included before you charge more.
- Who owns the finished Chronicle, and what you keep.
- Hosting term, renewal price, and what happens when it lapses.
- Cancellation, by either side, and the refund position.
- The succession and transfer clause.
- What happens if the commissioning person dies mid-project.
- Limitation of liability.

**The dangerous one is scope.** A heritage project has no natural end. A family
will keep remembering things. Without a written line, your sixty-hour build
becomes a two-hundred-hour build at the same price, and your margin was fiction.

---

## 3. Product repeatability

Said three times already this session and still true.

**There is no template.** A second Chronicle today means copying files by hand
out of a repository containing your father's biography, your mother's love
letter and three cloned voices. That is not a starting point you can hand
anybody, and it is not a process you can repeat under time pressure.

Until it exists:
- Every build costs full effort, so the margin in the plan is unproven.
- You cannot delegate any part of it.
- The isolation promise in the governance pack is a claim, not a mechanism.

**It needs nothing from you.** No client, no lawyer, no money. It is the highest
value thing you can do, and it is pure build.

## 4. Brand and presence

You are selling permanent web addresses to families, and **you do not have one.**

Missing:

| Item | Note |
|---|---|
| A company name you have actually chosen | "The Chronicle Company" is my coinage, not your decision |
| The domain for it | Buy it the day you choose the name |
| A business email address | A personal address undercuts a five-figure quote |
| A one-page site | Who you are, what a Chronicle is, the three tiers, how to start |
| A way to be contacted | Enquiry route that is not a personal phone number |

A family about to hand you their father's life, their mother's letters and
five thousand dollars will look you up. Right now there is nothing to find.

**This is also the cheapest marketing you will ever do**, because you already
know how to build exactly this kind of site.

## 5. Proof assets

Partly blocked by vertical 1 of the governance pack.

| Asset | State |
|---|---|
| A reference Chronicle | Exists, and is excellent |
| **Written permission to show it** | **Missing. Without it the main asset is unusable** |
| A one-page case study | Not written |
| A 60 to 90 second recording with the narration playing | Not made |
| A redacted sample source record | Not made. This is the one that proves the integrity rule physically |
| A written offer document, three tiers | Not made |

The redacted source record is the most underrated item on this list. Anyone can
claim they do not invent things. Showing a family the actual file, with the
gaps you could not fill, proves it in a way no sentence does.

**Done.** Eleven files, drafted 25 October 2026. Three items inside it still

The client agreement is vertical 2. The legal review covers both.

## 7. Delivery operations

Nothing written. Survivable for client one, painful by client three.

- An intake form: what you need from a family before you quote.
- An interview kit: the questions, in order, and how you record the answers.
- A way to track several commissions at once, with their stages and deadlines.
- A file handling routine: where a family's originals live, how they reach you,
  how they are named, and who may open them.
- **Measured hours.** The entire financial plan rests on an unmeasured guess.
  Measure the next build to the hour.

---

## 8. Insurance

Professional indemnity cover, before the first paid commission.

You will publish factual claims about real and in some cases prominent people,
handle private family material, and recreate living people's voices. The fee on
one Chronicle will not cover one letter from a lawyer.

Some corporate clients will also ask whether you carry it before signing.

---

## 9. The care product

The recurring fee is the enterprise value of this business, and it is currently
three words in a pricing table.

Define it, because a client will ask what they are paying 400 to 800 dollars a
year for:

- What is included: hosting, the domain, backups, uptime, small changes.
- **How many changes**, and how small is small.
- How fast you answer, and when you are unavailable.
- What costs extra.
- What happens if they stop paying. Does it come down, go read-only, or get
  exported to them?
- How they get their archive out, at any time, without asking permission.

Undefined aftercare turns a profitable retainer into unlimited free work.
