# Storm, Savannah and Beast Lords: what to draw next

## The problem worth solving first

You now have **two fundamentally different kinds of god**, and no way to tell
them apart on the page.

Ra is a **World Titan**. He came from outside, he forged his children
deliberately, and his line shows itself in anatomy: scales, feathers, wings,
horns. Inherited, physical, visible from birth.

The Storm God is a **Prime God born from the world itself**. Its blessing is
not ancestry, it is a relationship. Lightning, thunder, wind, endurance.
Granted, not inherited, and therefore revocable.

Put a dragon-blooded easterner and a storm-blessed northerner side by side
today, and nothing on the page tells you which divinity made them. Settle that
grammar in one image and every picture you make afterwards inherits it.

**My proposal for the rule:**

| | Ra's line, Titan-born | Storm-blessed, Prime-granted |
|---|---|---|
| Where it shows | in the body, from birth | on the body, and only when called |
| Form | scale, feather, horn, wing | light under the skin, in the eyes, along old scars |
| Permanence | cannot be lost | can be withdrawn |
| At rest | still visible | completely absent, an ordinary person |

That last row is the good one. **A storm-blessed warrior at rest looks like
nobody.** The god is not in them, it is with them, and it can leave. That is a
far more frightening kind of power to carry than scales, and it is the thing
that makes the northern peoples tragic rather than brutish.

## What to draw, in order

### 1. The Storm God
A Prime God born of the world, not forged like Ra's children. It should not
read as a sky-father with a beard and a hammer, and it should not look
designed, because nothing designed it. Weather that has become a will. The
most important single decision in the new lore, and nothing in your world
looks like it yet.

### 2. The grammar plate: Titan-born beside Prime-blessed
One image, two figures, standing together. A dragon-blooded easterner with the
inheritance in their body, and a storm-blessed northerner with the blessing
absent because they have not called it. This is a reference sheet, not a
scene, and it saves you from a hundred later inconsistencies.

### 3. A Beast Lord
Your most original concept and currently your least served, borrowing Godrian
Greyhide's portrait. Vikings and savannah warriors are things a reader can
already picture. A sovereign feral civilisation cannot be pictured until you
show it. See the continuity question below before drawing.

### 4. The contested grassland
Not a battle. The **cause**. Northern settlers' fences and ploughed ground
laid across a Beast Lord migration corridor, with the old trail still visible
underneath. One frame that explains a centuries-old war without a single
sword, and it refuses to tell the viewer who is right.

### 5. A savannah court in session
You explicitly warned against reducing this to Vikings versus tribal warriors.
The way to prevent that in art is to draw their **institutions**, not their
warriors. A monarch in council, scholars, records, law being argued. Draw the
warrior second, once the civilisation is established.

### 6. A northern clan assembly
Same reasoning, same correction. The oath-binding, the assembly, the
arbitration. Their legal tradition, not their axes. If the first northern
image you make is a man with a glowing axe, that is what they will be forever.

### 7. Southern jungle Elves
The fourth sphere. They need to look clearly distinct from the Fae realms you
have already drawn, or the southern map reads as one undifferentiated green.

## Two images that would be better than any of the above

These are not on the list because they are harder, but they are worth more.

**The Storm God refusing a northern king.** Your own line: a Prime God charged
with planetary balance may have very different priorities from the mortals
invoking its name. One picture of the god declining to bless a war does more
for the northern peoples than ten pictures of them winning one, because it
makes them people who can be wrong rather than villains who are wrong.

**A Beast Lord ruler choosing their own species over their human allies.** You
named this tension yourself. It is the moment the alliance stops being
decorative. It also proves they are sovereign rather than auxiliary, which is
the single thing most likely to get lost in the art.

## Continuity question before any Beast Lord is drawn

Your site already says this about them, in `content.js`:

> *"Shaped to cull corruption, and never asked whether they wished to be."*
> *"The Elu-Dues and the beast kin who answered the Union."*

The new lore says they are *"independent sovereign peoples"* in *"an ancient
relationship between equals"* with the savannah kingdoms.

Made as instruments, and now sovereign equals. Those are not incompatible, and
in fact the gap between them is a better story than either on its own: a people
built as a tool who became a nation, allied with the only humans who treated
them as such from the beginning. But it needs confirming before it is drawn,
because it changes everything about how they carry themselves.

And it raises the question the lore has not answered: **who shaped them, and
were they ever released?**

## Craft rules, unchanged

Everything in `docs/dragon-god-prompts.md` still applies: one named light
source, three colours, large areas with no detail, a readable silhouette, a
mortal in frame for scale, and none of the banned words. Generate all of these
with `BOI-animation/keyframes/clash-v2.png` as a style reference so the two new
continents look like they belong to the same world as the elves.
