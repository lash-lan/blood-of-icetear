# What the market will pay for a Chronicle, and why

Market research done 17 October 2026. Every figure below comes from a public
source, and the sources are listed at the end.

**Read this caveat first.** Most pricing pages on the internet for this kind of
work are written by the companies selling it, or by affiliate blogs comparing
them. The numbers are indicative, not audited. Where sources disagree, both
figures are given rather than averaged. Nothing here is a quote.

---

## 1. The single most important finding

**There are two completely separate markets, priced about a hundred times
apart, and which one you appear to be in decides what you can charge.**

### Market A: memorial websites. A commodity.
| Service | Price |
|---|---|
| Online-Tribute | 95 US dollars one-off, or 8 US dollars a month |
| Keeper Plus | 75 to 99 US dollars one-off |
| Legacy.com | 49 US dollars first year, then 19 a year |
| UK monthly plans generally | 4 to 12 pounds a month |
| Free tiers | Free, usually with advertising on the page |

These are templates. You fill in a form. Some of the free ones put adverts
beside a dead person's photograph.

### Market B: commissioned life stories. A luxury.
| Service | Price |
|---|---|
| StoryTerrace, entry package | from 3,250 US dollars |
| StoryTerrace, professional tier | from 24,000 US dollars |
| LifeBook Memoirs | 9,000 to 42,000 US dollars, depending on source |
| Memoir ghostwriting, Reedsy 2026 data | 18,000 to 50,000 US dollars |
| Professional genealogy, hourly | 90 to 250 US dollars an hour |
| Multi-generational family history with archival work | 20,000 US dollars and up |
| Legacy Tree, top package | 48,750 US dollars |
| Corporate founder or executive tribute book | 10,000 to 50,000 US dollars |

**These are all books.** Paper. No audio, no film, no live web address, nothing
the family can add to afterwards.

### Where the Chronicle actually sits
It has the **depth, research discipline and deliverable quality of Market B**,
and it has been accidentally described in the language of Market A, because it
is "a website".

That is the single most expensive mistake available here. A Chronicle is a
**commissioned private biography that happens to be delivered as a living web
archive instead of a printed book.** Priced as a website it is worth two
thousand. Positioned as a commissioned biography it is worth ten.

## 2. What bespoke web work alone commands

For reference, ignoring the heritage angle entirely, the market rate for a
genuinely custom one-page site with real motion design:

| Tier | Price |
|---|---|
| Freelancer, single-scroll one-page site | 800 to 2,500 US dollars |
| Agency, small custom site | 3,000 to 15,000 US dollars |
| Boutique landing page, 1 to 5 pages | 3,000 to 10,000 US dollars |
| Premium package with bespoke motion design | 20,000 to 60,000 US dollars and up |
| Agency hourly, US, Canada, Australia | 100 to 149 US dollars an hour |

Two notes that matter. A fully custom build runs **two to four times** the price
of a template-based one. And **animation is normally billed as a separate line
item**, because it is the most labour-intensive part of a site.

The Chronicle's opening film and scroll-directed photography is exactly that
line item, and it is already built.

## 3. The Malaysian problem

Local rates, from 2026 Malaysian agency sources:

| Tier | Price |
|---|---|
| Template corporate package | from RM 1,999 |
| Freelancer business website | RM 2,000 to 8,000 |
| SME corporate site, 6 to 10 pages | RM 6,000 to 14,000 |
| Corporate site, 10 to 20 pages | RM 8,000 to 20,000 |
| Multinational, bilingual, 20 to 30 pages | RM 18,000 to 45,000 |
| Annual hosting and maintenance, charged by agencies | RM 1,200 to 6,000 a year |

So a Malaysian buyer's instinct for "a website about my father" will anchor
somewhere around **RM 8,000 to 20,000**, which is roughly 1,800 to 4,500 US
dollars. That is the ceiling of the local web market and well below the floor of
the global biography market.

**Two conclusions.**

First, that RM 1,200 to 6,000 a year that local agencies charge for hosting and
maintenance is the number to remember. It is the normal, expected, unquestioned
line on every Malaysian web invoice, and the real cost of running a Chronicle is
about **RM 70 a year**. That gap is the business.

Second, the serious money is not local. It is the **diaspora**: Malaysian, Sri
Lankan, Indian and Chinese families whose successful children live in Singapore,
London, Sydney, Dubai, Toronto or California, earn in those currencies, and feel
guilty about distance from an ageing parent. They buy in US dollars and pounds.

## 4. Recommended pricing

Three tiers, two currencies. The local column is for a Malaysian family paying
in ringgit. The export column is for the diaspora child paying in a strong
currency.

### Tier 1: The Chronicle
One person's life. Full research and source record, the written story, the
photograph archive, the opening film, the still site, read aloud in a
professional narrator's voice, the private reader dashboard, and the family's own
editing handover.

**RM 14,000 to 22,000** locally. **5,500 to 9,000 US dollars** export.

Justification: it is StoryTerrace's entry tier with more delivered, and it is
priced below LifeBook's floor.

### Tier 2: The Chronicle, with voices
Tier 1, plus the subject's own voice and those of named family members, cloned
from recordings they make and consent to, reading their own words.

**Add RM 4,000 to 7,000**, or **1,800 to 3,000 US dollars**, per voice.

Justification: this is the thing nobody else sells, it cannot be bought later if
the person dies, and it is the single most emotionally powerful feature in the
product. Price it per voice, sold with written consent only.

### Tier 3: The Family Archive
Several Chronicles, the family tree joining them, a shared photograph archive,
and a structure the family keeps adding to.

**RM 35,000 and up**, or **15,000 US dollars and up**, scoped per family.

Justification: competes with the 20,000 dollar multi-generational family history
and the 48,750 dollar genealogy package, and beats both because it is living
rather than printed.

### The recurring fee, which is the actual business
**Custody and care: RM 1,200 to 2,400 a year, or 400 to 800 US dollars a year.**

Covers the web address, hosting, backups, uptime, small text and photograph
changes, and the written succession arrangement.

True cost: about **15 US dollars a year.** Malaysian agencies already charge
RM 1,200 to 6,000 a year for exactly this line, so it is expected rather than
resented. Thirty Chronicles on care is RM 36,000 to 72,000 a year of recurring
income for a few hours of work.

### Corporate: The Founder Chronicle
The same product, sold to a company for a founder, a retiring chief executive,
or a company anniversary. Corporate buyers have a budget line and no emotional
hesitation about price.

**8,000 to 25,000 US dollars.**

Justification: the market pays 10,000 to 50,000 dollars for a founder tribute
**book**, and companies are now commissioning these instead of giving a
retirement gift. A living, narrated web archive is a better artefact than a book
nobody opens, and it can be shared with every employee and client by sending a
link.

## 5. The justification, in the words to actually use

Five arguments, strongest first.

### 1. It is not a website, it is a commissioned biography
Anchor every conversation against the 3,250 to 42,000 dollar memoir market, and
never against the 95 dollar memorial template. If a client compares you to
ForeverMissed, the positioning has failed, not the price.

### 2. Nothing is invented, and that costs money
The research, the sourcing record, the verification levels on every photograph
and the refusal to write one unverifiable sentence is **the majority of the
labour** and the entire reason the family can trust it. Professional
genealogists charge 90 to 250 dollars an hour for precisely this. Say so.

A competitor using AI to generate a warm, plausible, invented paragraph about
someone's father is selling a forgery. That is the whole pitch.

### 3. The voice cannot be bought later
A living relative can record themselves today. In ten years they may not be able
to. This is genuinely, permanently time-limited, and it is the most honest
urgency any product can have. **Never manufacture it, never use it on a grieving
family, and never clone anyone who has not personally agreed.** Used cleanly, it
is unanswerable.

### 4. The occasion carries the budget
Nobody wakes up wanting a heritage website. They want something for a 60th,
70th or 80th birthday, a retirement, a 50th wedding anniversary, a company
milestone, or a diagnosis. Those moments already have money allocated, a hard
deadline, and several relatives willing to split the cost.

Note from the corporate research: start **twelve to eighteen months** before an
anniversary date. Sell against the calendar, not against need.

### 5. It is a living archive, not a finished artefact
The book is done the day it prints. A Chronicle is added to for twenty years:
new photographs, new Chronicles for other relatives, the next generation's
letters. The family can do it themselves, which is why they trust it, and the
care fee is why you still get paid.

---

## 6. Honest risks

- **There is no proof yet.** One site, one family, no paying client, no
  testimonial, no portfolio. The first two sales will be cheaper than they
  should be, and that is normal. Do not let the first price become the price.
- **The word "website" costs about eighty percent of the value.** Every piece of
  marketing language has to carry Chronicle, archive, or commission instead.
- **Nothing is reusable yet.** Until the starter template exists, every site
  costs full effort, so the early margin is bad whatever the price says.
- **Consent is the business risk, not a formality.** Living people, private
  letters, cloned voices. One family dispute without signed consent ends the
  venture.
- **The research above is mostly vendor-published.** Before quoting anyone
  seriously, get two real quotes from StoryTerrace and LifeBook and see what
  they actually say in writing.
