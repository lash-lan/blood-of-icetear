# Continuity, ownership and succession

The risk ordinary AI checklists miss, and the one that cuts deepest here,
because the product's entire promise is permanence.

---

## The contradiction to resolve

A heritage business whose clients' heritage depends on that business surviving
has a contradiction at its centre. It must be answered in the contract, before
the first signature.

**The position taken:** the company holds the domain and account during the
build and the first care year, under a written and **tested** transfer path,
with a dead-man clause naming who inherits. Every client can leave at any time,
with everything, without needing the company's cooperation to keep their
Chronicle alive.

## The questions every client agreement answers

- Who owns the domain and the site files?
- Who may administer the account, and who inherits it?
- Who controls publication after the commissioning person dies?
- How long is hosting included, and what does renewal cost?
- What happens when a subscription lapses?
- Can the family export the complete archive, and how?
- Do AI-generated components still work if a vendor disappears?
- What happens to private data after termination?
- Can a family appoint a successor administrator?
- What happens if the company is sold or closed?

## Never sell preservation forever

Unless the company can actually support that commitment, the words are not used.

What is sold instead: **a defined hosting period, renewable care, and a portable
export the family can take anywhere.**

## The export

Every client receives, on request and at the end of any engagement:

- Every original photograph, recording and document they supplied.
- The complete written content as plain text.
- The published site as plain files.
- The narration audio.
- The source and fact record.
- Their own copies of their consent forms.

Kept separate from the export: the company's reusable templates and platform
code, which are not the client's.

## On closure

If the company closes, every client receives a complete export and at least
[N] months' notice before anything is taken down. This obligation is written
into the client agreement so that it survives the decision to close.

## The reproducibility rule

**Everything published must be rebuildable from committed, versioned sources.**

This is a continuity control, not a tidiness preference. Two existing failures
show why:

1. The voice pipeline exists only on one laptop, in an unversioned folder,
   needing a specific environment and a locally started server. If that machine
   fails, no new narration can ever be made, for any client.
2. On the companion project, fifteen chapters of published audio exist that no
   committed script can regenerate.

**Required before the first paying commission:** the voice pipeline, including
the source recordings, moved into a private versioned repository with the
recordings held under the Confidential rules in file 04.

## Succession for the company itself

A written handover naming where the domain, the repositories, the secrets, the
backups and the client agreements live, and who inherits them. Kept with the
client agreements. Reviewed annually.
