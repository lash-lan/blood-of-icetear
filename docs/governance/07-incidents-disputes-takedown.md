# Incidents, disputes and takedown

Three different failures, three different procedures. All three are written
before they are needed, because none of them can be designed during.

---

## 1. Takedown

Anyone featured, or their guardian, may ask for material to be removed.

**The rule: it comes down first, and is discussed afterwards.** There is no
review period during which contested material about a real person stays up.

| Step | Action | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Acknowledge the request in writing | Same working day |
| 2 | Unpublish the material | Within 1 working day |
| 3 | Preserve the evidence and the request. Do not destroy anything | Immediately |
| 4 | Tell the commissioning family what was removed and why | Within 2 working days |
| 5 | Resolve, or escalate under section 2 | As long as it takes |

Material stays down while anything is unresolved.

## 2. Family disputes

A Chronicle involves several people with competing interests. The company is not
the judge of a family's history, and must never be manoeuvred into acting like
one.

Situations that will occur:

- One sibling commissions, another objects to publishing a private story.
- The client wants to include an ex-spouse's photograph and history.
- A parent uploads photographs of children who cannot meaningfully consent.
- A deceased person's recorded wishes conflict with what relatives want.
- A relative asks to remove something embarrassing but true.
- The client dies before the Chronicle is finished.
- The subject withdraws consent after publication.

**The policy in four steps:**

1. **Pause** publication of the contested material.
2. **Preserve** the material, the sources and the correspondence.
3. **Ask** the parties to clarify, in writing, who authorises what.
4. **Escalate** rather than decide. Where the family cannot agree, the material
   stays out. Absence is survivable. A wrong adjudication is not.

**Withdrawal after publication is always honoured.** A signature given once does
not bind a person forever.

**Where the client dies mid-project**, work stops until the succession clause in
file 09 identifies who may now instruct.

## 3. Security incidents

A heritage archive holds identity documents, private letters, photographs of
children and reusable voice samples. It is worth stealing.

**On discovering an incident:**

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Contain. Revoke keys, close access, stop the bleeding |
| 2 | Record the time of discovery. The clock starts here |
| 3 | Investigate scope: whose data, which assets, how |
| 4 | Preserve evidence and logs |
| 5 | Assess whether it is notifiable |
| 6 | Notify as required, and tell affected people in plain language |
| 7 | Remediate, then write down what changed |

**Verified 25 October 2026.** Malaysia's amended data protection regime carries
a breach notification duty to the Commissioner, with secondary guidance widely
reporting a **72-hour** window from awareness where the criteria are met, framed
around significant harm or significant scale. **Needs legal confirmation** of
the exact triggers and of notification duties to affected individuals.

**The posture:** assess every incident for reportability. Do not assume that
every incident is notifiable, and do not assume that none is.

**Voice-specific incidents** are treated as severe by default. If a voice model
or source recording is exposed, the person whose voice it is must be told, the
model destroyed and regenerated if they still want it, and the vendor asked what
they hold.
