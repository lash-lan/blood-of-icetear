# Vendors and cross-border transfers

Every service that touches a client's material is another place that material
now lives.

---

## The rule

**Do not paste a family's archive into an AI tool because the tool produces
attractive results.**

Use the minimum necessary material. Redact what is not needed. Prefer business
arrangements with written terms over consumer sign-ups.

**Never sent to any general AI tool:** identity documents, signed consent forms,
original voice recordings, medical or financial detail, and anything classified
Confidential in file 04.

## The assessment, before a vendor is approved

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Does the vendor train on uploaded data? | Secondary use of a family's private material |
| Where is it stored and processed? | Cross-border requirements |
| How long is it retained? | Deletion and withdrawal promises |
| Are subcontractors involved? | Processing you cannot see |
| Can data and voice models be deleted on request? | Withdrawal has to be real |
| Are commercial rights granted to the output? | Whether the deliverable is lawfully the client's |
| Are there written security commitments? | Accountability and recourse |
| Can access be restricted and audited? | Finding and proving misuse |
| What happens if they close or change terms? | Continuity of a client's Chronicle |

**An advertised privacy feature is not a contractual guarantee.** Check the
actual plan, the actual terms and the actual settings. A marketing page is not
a commitment.

## Cross-border transfers

Client material will be processed outside Malaysia by most of the vendors above.

**Needs legal confirmation.** Malaysian requirements for transferring personal
data abroad must be confirmed against the Commissioner's current guidance before
the first paying commission, and the privacy notice must name the categories of
recipient.

**Where the client, the subject or the audience is in the EU**, assess the EU AI
Act's role-based duties as well. **Verified 25 October 2026:** Article 50
transparency obligations have applied since 2 August 2026, with machine-readable
marking of pre-existing generative systems due by 2 December 2026, and penalties
reported up to 15 million euro or 3 percent of worldwide annual turnover. The
duties fall differently on providers and deployers, and extend to operators
outside the EU whose output reaches EU users.

## Treating client uploads as untrusted

A document a paying client uploads is still untrusted input to any AI that reads
it. A file can carry instructions aimed at the model rather than content aimed
at a reader.

Controls: validate file types, scan uploads, never let a document's own text
alter the workflow, keep one client's material strictly out of another's
context, and review AI output for content that did not come from the approved
sources.
