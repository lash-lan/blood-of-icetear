# The Five Dragon Gods, as prompts

Ra, Father of Skies, forged Osiris, Tiamatra, Bahamut, Ryujin and Delphyne.
From them came the first Dragons and Phoenixes. These five need to look like
the origin of every winged thing that follows, which means they cannot look
like five variations of the same dragon.

---

## Why the first attempts came out generic

Not too much detail. **Uniformly** too much detail. Every square inch of those
five images is at maximum sharpness, maximum contrast and maximum saturation,
so the eye has nowhere to rest and nothing reads as important. A god needs a
quiet frame around it, the way a cathedral needs empty floor.

Five specific faults, all fixable in the prompt:

1. **No silhouette.** Shrink any of them to thumbnail size and you cannot tell
   what you are looking at. The bone dragon in the cavern is an unreadable pile
   of spikes. Card art has to survive being small.
2. **The background is doing the work.** God rays, glowing runes, mist, a sun
   directly behind the head, a spired city below. Those five ingredients appear
   in every image, which is exactly why the dragons feel interchangeable.
3. **Complementary colours at full strength.** Red and blue both at maximum
   cancel each other out and read as noise, not as fire.
4. **No scale.** Nothing human-sized anywhere, so nothing feels enormous. Size
   is a comparison, never an adjective.
5. **No story beat.** They are dragons, not gods carrying something.

### Words to stop using

`masterpiece` · `best quality` · `ultra detailed` · `hyper detailed` ·
`intricate` · `8k` · `octane render` · `trending on artstation` ·
`epic` · `majestic` · `glowing runes` · `volumetric god rays`

Every one of those pushes the model toward uniform maximum detail. They are the
cause, not the cure.

### Words to use instead

Name the **light source**, the **palette**, the **silhouette**, the **rest
areas** and the **one thing happening**. Then say what to leave out.

---

## The scaffold

Each prompt below follows the same six-part shape. Reuse it for anything else
in the setting.

```
1. WHAT IT IS       anatomy, specific, three or four features only
2. WHAT IT IS DOING one action or one moment, never a pose
3. THE SHOT         camera distance, angle, where the figure sits in frame
4. LIGHT            one source, named, with a direction
5. PALETTE + REST   three colours maximum, and what stays quiet
6. SCALE            something small and known, for comparison
```

## 1. Osiris — Sacred Fire, the Rebirth

> A feathered dragon god seen from below against a pale dawn sky, wings half
> spread, head turned down toward the viewer. Its body is covered in long
> overlapping feathers rather than scales, deep ember red along the back fading
> to ash grey at the wingtips, with a throat and chest of pale bone white.
>
> It is the moment of ignition: fire is beginning at the feather tips and
> travelling inward along the wings, red flame on the left wing and cold blue
> flame on the right, the two meeting at the breast. The feathers it has already
> burned are falling as grey ash.
>
> Low angle, the god filling the upper two thirds, the lower third empty pale
> sky and drifting ash. Single light source: the fire itself, from inside the
> wings, so the underside of the body is lit and the back is in shadow against
> the sky.
>
> Three colours only: ember red, cold blue, bone white, on grey. Large areas of
> flat unbroken sky. Detail concentrated only at the breast where the two fires
> meet; the wingtips dissolve into ash and are barely drawn.
>
> Scale: a single small bird, far below and tiny, for comparison.
>
> Painted fantasy illustration, calm and ceremonial rather than violent.
> Avoid: sunbursts behind the head, lava, glowing runes, a city below.

## 2. Tiamatra — the Queen, Burdened

> A four limbed western dragon queen, enormous, settling onto a mountain ridge
> with her wings still half open. Heavy armoured scale in dark iron grey, a
> crest of bone horns swept back like a crown, and old scars across her chest
> that have healed pale.
>
> She has just landed and she is looking down at something below her out of
> frame. Her head is lowered and turned away from the viewer. This is grief
> held still, not a roar.
>
> Three quarter rear view so the viewer stands behind and beneath her, the way
> a subject stands behind a monarch. She occupies the right half of the frame.
> The left half is open sky and the drop of the valley.
>
> Single light source: flat overcast daylight from the left, no sun visible.
> Her far side falls into shadow.
>
> Palette: iron grey, dull gold at the horns only, and one note of deep red in
> the membrane of the wings when the light passes through them. The sky is a
> single flat grey. Detail only on the head and the scarred chest; the tail and
> the far wing are simplified almost to silhouette.
>
> Scale: a ruined watchtower on the ridge beside her, reaching only to her
> forelimb.
>
> Painted fantasy illustration, restrained, heavy, quiet.
> Avoid: fire, open mouth, sunset colours, glowing eyes, mist machines.

---

## 3. Bahamut — the Fallen, Corrupted

> A once glorious dragon god, now wrong. The frame is mostly his wings, black
> and vast, but the membrane has gone thin and torn in places like rotted sail
> cloth. Where the corruption has taken hold the black is split by seams of wet
> blue black, as though the sea had got inside him and was pushing out.
>
> He is half submerged, standing in shallow black water on a drowned plain, his
> head bowed so low it almost touches the surface. He is looking at his own
> reflection. That is the whole picture: a god looking at what he has become.
>
> Eye level with the water, flat horizon, the god centred and symmetrical like
> an altarpiece. His reflection takes the lower third and is darker and less
> detailed than he is.
>
> Single light source: a cold grey break in the cloud behind him, so he is
> largely a silhouette and only the wet edges of him catch light.
>
> Palette: black, wet grey, and one sickly blue in the corruption seams. Nothing
> else. At least half the frame is near black with no detail in it at all.
>
> Scale: a drowned stone archway to one side, waist deep, the top of it level
> with his ankle.
>
> Painted fantasy illustration. Tragic, still, funereal. He should look like he
> was beautiful once and knows he is not.
> Avoid: fire, lava, red, spikes everywhere, action poses, a roaring mouth.

---

## 4. Ryujin — the Long One, Wisdom and Water

> A long eastern dragon, serpentine, no wings, four short limbs tucked close to
> the body, a mane and whiskers of fine white hair streaming behind. Pale jade
> and white scales, small and smooth, more like a koi than a lizard.
>
> He is moving through the air above still water, and the air is behaving like
> water: his passage leaves a slow curling wake behind him and the rain is
> falling upward into it. He is not fighting anything. He is passing through,
> and the world rearranges politely around him.
>
> Wide shot, the body entering from the left and curving across the frame in one
> long unbroken S, head near the right edge, three quarters of his length
> visible. He is small in the frame. The emptiness is the point.
>
> Single light source: diffuse pale light from directly above, through cloud,
> no sun disc.
>
> Palette: pale jade, white, and the near black of the still water below.
> Nothing saturated anywhere. The upper half of the frame is almost empty sky.
> Detail only on the head and the first third of the body; the rest simplifies
> into a clean ribbon.
>
> Scale: a single fishing boat on the water far below, the length of one of his
> whiskers.
>
> Painted fantasy illustration in the manner of ink and wash, elegant, divine,
> unhurried. Not beastly, not threatening.
> Avoid: western dragon anatomy, wings, horns like antlers, sparkles, particle
> effects, glowing orbs, pagodas, cherry blossom.

---

## 5. Delphyne — the Wingspan, Prophecy and Wind

> A dragon of almost nothing but wing. The span is so wide that both tips leave
> the frame, and the body between them is slender and small, almost an
> afterthought. Feather and membrane together, the colour of weathered canvas
> and cloud.
>
> She is riding a standing wave of wind at great altitude and not flapping. She
> is holding completely still while everything around her moves, and her brood
> of young wyverns is strung out behind and below her in a long ragged line,
> each one much smaller, fighting the air she is resting in.
>
> Extreme wide shot from level with her, the horizon curved and very low in the
> frame, so most of the picture is open air. She crosses the upper third
> horizontally. Vast emptiness below.
>
> Single light source: hard high sun from behind and above, so she is lit along
> the top edge only and the underside of the wings is in deep shadow.
>
> Palette: cloud white, canvas grey, deep shadow blue. No warm colour at all.
> Most of the frame is empty graded sky with no detail whatsoever.
>
> Scale: the wyverns behind her, each small enough to sit on one of her
> fingers.
>
> Painted fantasy illustration, sweeping, remote, untouchable. The feeling is
> altitude and silence.
> Avoid: clutter, cities, mountains close by, storms, lightning, dramatic
> clouds, anything that fills the empty space.

## If one comes out over-rendered anyway

Add this line at the end and run it again:

> Large areas of the frame carry no detail at all. Soft edges everywhere except
> the single focal point. Flat broad shapes, not picked out textures.

And remove whatever word in your own prompt is asking for density. It is
usually `ornate`, `covered in`, `every scale`, or `detailed`.
