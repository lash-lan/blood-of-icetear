## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### The business
Commissioned private biographies, delivered as living web archives rather than printed books. Each one is called a **Chronicle**: one person's life, researched, written, illustrated with their own photographs, narrated aloud in a real voice, published at a permanent web address, and editable by the family forever afterwards.

### The insight
There are two markets for this, priced roughly a hundred times apart.

**Memorial websites** are a commodity: 49 to 99 US dollars one-off, or 4 to 12 pounds a month, for a fill-in-the-blanks template. Some free tiers display advertising beside a dead person's photograph.

**Commissioned life stories** are a luxury: 3,250 to 50,000 US dollars for a ghostwritten memoir or a researched family history. Every single one of those is a printed book. None has audio, none has motion, none has a live address, and none can be added to after it ships.

The Chronicle has the second market's depth and the first market's delivery cost. It is positioned, priced and sold as the second.

### The offer
| Tier | Export price (USD) | Local price (MYR) |
|---|---|---|
| The Chronicle | 5,500 to 9,000 | 14,000 to 22,000 |
| Add a cloned voice, per person | 1,800 to 3,000 | 4,000 to 7,000 |
| The Family Archive, multiple Chronicles | 15,000+ | 35,000+ |
| The Founder Chronicle, corporate | 8,000 to 25,000 | priced in USD |
| Custody and care, annual | 400 to 800 /year | 1,200 to 2,400 /year |

### The economics
Infrastructure cost per client site: **about 15 US dollars a year.** Gross margin on a Chronicle is above 95 percent before the author's own time. The recurring care fee is the real business: thirty sites under care is 12,000 to 24,000 US dollars a year for a few hours of monthly work.

### The ask of this plan
Validate or destroy three assumptions: that families will pay biography prices for a web deliverable, that one person can deliver eight to twelve Chronicles a year, and that the consent framework is sufficient to publish living people's faces, letters and cloned voices commercially.

## 2. THE PRODUCT

### 2.1 What a Chronicle is

A single long page telling one person's life in a fixed emotional order:

> the name, the journey, the man or woman, the spouse, the children, the wider family, the friends and the mentored, what they built, what they believe, the legacy, the occasion, the future.

A visitor should arrive impressed by an accomplished person and leave feeling they know a human being.

### 2.2 The five things a competitor cannot copy quickly

**1. Nothing is invented.** Not one quotation, memory, date, award or relationship. Where a fact is not known, the page shows a quiet, marked gap that renders as nothing until the family fills it. Every fact has a source recorded in a private file that the reader never sees. An unsourceable fact does not ship at all, because there is no hedging sentence left to carry it.

This is the central promise. It is also the main cost, and the main defence against AI-generated competitors, who will cheerfully produce a beautiful invented paragraph about a stranger's father.

**2. It reads itself aloud.** A narrator's voice reads the whole page, highlighting each line and scrolling the page to follow, over a music bed that ducks under the words. An eighty-year-old relative presses one button.

**3. It opens with a film.** The family's own photographs, directed by the visitor's scroll. Scroll is the clock, so scrolling back rewinds it exactly. It never hijacks the scroll and it never performs. It reveals.

**4. The family edits it themselves.** All the writing lives in one plain text file with a plain-English guide. Adding a grandchild's letter or fifty archive photographs never requires a developer again.

**5. Total visitor privacy.** No tracking script, no cookie, no third-party anything on any page. The owner still gets reader numbers, because they are read from the hosting provider's own aggregated traffic data rather than collected from visitors.

### 2.3 What it is not

Not a memorial site. The reference build is for a **living** man, as a birthday tribute, and nothing on it reads like a memorial. The product serves living subjects first. That is both a differentiator and an ethical boundary: a living person can consent, record their own voice and correct the record. A dead one cannot.

Two findings matter. A fully custom build costs **two to four times** a template-based one. And **motion design is normally billed as a separate line item** because it is the most labour-intensive part of a site. The Chronicle's scroll-directed film is exactly that line item, and it is already engineered.

### 3.4 The home market, and its ceiling

Malaysian agency rates, 2026:

| Tier | Price |
|---|---|
| Template corporate package | from RM 1,999 |
| Freelance business website | RM 2,000 to 8,000 |
| SME corporate site, 6 to 10 pages | RM 6,000 to 14,000 |
| Corporate site, 10 to 20 pages | RM 8,000 to 20,000 |
| Multinational, bilingual, 20 to 30 pages | RM 18,000 to 45,000 |
| **Annual hosting and maintenance, charged by agencies** | **RM 1,200 to 6,000 a year** |

A Malaysian buyer's instinct for "a website about my father" anchors near **RM 8,000 to 20,000**, roughly 1,800 to 4,500 USD. That is the top of the local web market and below the floor of the global biography market.

Two strategic conclusions follow.

**First, the annual maintenance line is already normalised locally at RM 1,200 to 6,000.** The true cost of running a Chronicle is about RM 70 a year. The care fee is therefore expected rather than resented, and it is pure margin.

**Second, the serious money is not local.** It is the diaspora.

### 3.5 The buyer

**The buyer is not the subject.** The subject is an ageing parent. The buyer is their successful adult child, typically 35 to 55, living in Singapore, London, Sydney, Dubai, Toronto or California, earning in a strong currency, carrying guilt about distance, and with a milestone approaching.

This person already buys 5,000 dollar watches as gifts. The Chronicle competes with those, not with a website.

### 3.6 The occasion

Nobody wakes up wanting a heritage website. They want something for:

- a 60th, 70th, 80th or 90th birthday
- a retirement
- a 40th or 50th wedding anniversary
- a company founding anniversary or a founder's departure
- a serious diagnosis

Each of these has money already allocated, a hard deadline, and several relatives willing to split the cost. The corporate research is explicit that heritage projects should begin **twelve to eighteen months** before an anniversary date, because research and interviews always take longer than expected. Sell against the calendar, not against need.

### 3.7 Competitive landscape summary

| Competitor type | Their weakness |
|---|---|
| Memorial platforms | Templates, self-service, no research, advertising on free tiers |
| Memoir ghostwriters | Printed only, no audio, no visuals, cannot be added to |
| Genealogy firms | Records and charts, not narrative or design |
| Web agencies | Beautiful, but will invent copy and have no sourcing discipline |
| AI memoir apps, 149 to 349 USD | Cheap, generic, and will fabricate. The direct threat to the category's credibility, and the strongest argument for the integrity rule |

**The gap:** nobody is selling a researched, sourced, narrated, living, family-maintainable archive. That is the whole opportunity.

## 8. DELIVERY PROCESS

Nine stages. Stage 1 is never skipped for any fee.

**Stage 1: Commission and consent.** Who the Chronicle is about, whether they are living, who else appears, what must never be published. Signed consent from every living person who appears, covering image, written words and voice separately. A written takedown route: anything contested comes down first and is discussed afterwards. Written agreement on ownership and on who hosts it after handover.

**Stage 2: Collect.** Interviews against a checklist. Photographs, documents, letters, professional record. Every fact gets a source the family can point at, recorded in a private file. An identity warning for the subject, because more than one person shares most names and one wrong internet fact destroys the entire promise.

**Stage 3: Write.** All writing into one data file. Every unknown left as a marked gap. Every photograph recorded with caption, people, owner, source, and a verification level of confirmed, approximate or unknown. Only name people the family has named.

**Stage 4: Photographs.** Automatic generation of three web sizes each, plus the film's backdrop versions.

**Stage 5: The still site.** Every chapter, finished and readable, with no animation whatsoever. Must work with scripts entirely disabled. This is the floor everything else stands on.

**Stage 6: The film.** Built on top, never instead.

**Stage 7: Narration.** Only once the words are final. The script is generated from the page's own content, so the voice can never read what the page no longer says, and the listen button hides itself automatically if the words later change.

**Stage 8: Verify, then present.** Laptop and phone, motion on and off, scripts on and off. Then the family. **Publish only on their word.** Then verify the real address rather than a preview.

**Stage 9: Handover.** How to edit their file, the dashboard password, where everything lives, and a written succession note naming who inherits the address and the account.

### The five rules that never bend
1. Never invent anything about a real person.
2. An unsourced fact does not ship.
3. Animation reveals, it never performs. The visitor always controls the scroll.
4. No tracker on any page, ever.
5. The family must be able to edit it without us.

## 9. TECHNICAL INFRASTRUCTURE, PER CLIENT

### 9.1 Architecture summary

Each client gets a **fully isolated stack**. Nothing is shared between client sites: not the code repository, not the hosting worker, not the domain, not the secrets, not the data. One client's material can never appear in another's site, and a breach of one reaches nothing else.

### 9.2 What is created per client

| Component | Service | Isolation |
|---|---|---|
| Private code repository, holding all content, photographs and audio | GitHub, private | One per client |
| Static site generator | Astro 6, static output, no adapter | Per repository |
| Hosting worker, serving the built files worldwide | Cloudflare Workers | One per client |
| Web address | Cloudflare Registrar | One per client |
| Test address | *.workers.dev, kept alive | One per client |
| Automatic publishing on change | Cloudflare Workers Builds, watching the main branch | One per client |
| Private reader dashboard | A small Worker route, password protected | One per client |
| Dashboard password | Cloudflare secret, STATS_PASS | One per client |
| Analytics read key, scoped to that one domain only | Cloudflare secret, CF_API_TOKEN | One per client |
| Reader statistics source | Cloudflare's own aggregated traffic data | Per domain |
| Backup | The GitHub repository itself, with full version history | Per client |

### 9.3 What is shared, and belongs to the business

| Component | Purpose |
|---|---|
| The starter template repository | The skeleton every Chronicle is cut from. **Does not yet exist. See Section 15** |
| The consent and takedown pack | Stage 1 paperwork |
| The scope and price document | Commercial terms |
| Claude Code subscription | Builds every site |
| ChatGPT subscription | Image generation and script polish |
| OpenRouter account | Narration audio and generated imagery |
| HeyGen account | Voice cloning, only when sold |

### 9.4 The technology, and why each piece

**Astro 6, static output.** Turns organised source files into finished pages in advance. The entire dependency list is one item: Astro itself. No framework, no UI library, no client-side router. Consequence: a site that loads fast, has almost no attack surface, and will still work in a decade without maintenance. For a product sold on permanence, this is a feature and not a shortcut.

**Cloudflare Workers with static assets.** When a visitor requests a file that exists, it is served directly from the nearest of roughly three hundred cities and **never touches any code**. Only the private dashboard routes execute anything. Consequence: almost nothing can break, and hosting is free at this scale.

**Cloudflare Workers Builds.** Watches the main branch. A change pushed is live in about five minutes. Nothing is ever uploaded by hand, which removes the single most common cause of a site being accidentally broken.

**A build that refuses to publish a mistake.** The build command runs a house-style check against the finished pages and **fails the build** if a banned character reaches anything a visitor can read. A failed build leaves the live site exactly as it was. The gate is a real engineering asset and should be extended to cover missing photographs and broken links.

**Cloudflare's own analytics, read through its data interface.** The counting happens at the hosting provider's edge, from traffic they already see. **No script on the page, no cookie on the visitor, no personal data stored anywhere.** The owner sees requests per day, unique visitors, top pages and top countries. Consequence: nothing to disclose to visitors, no consent banner, and a genuine privacy claim rather than a marketing one.

**The dashboard sign-in is a form, never the browser's password box.** Learned the hard way: in-app browsers, the kind inside messaging apps, often refuse to display the browser's own password dialog and substitute their own error page. Every response from the dashboard is a normal successful page with any problem written in the body, so no browser can substitute anything. This matters commercially, because a client's first attempt to view their numbers will usually be from a link in a message.

**Narration by hosted text-to-speech.** Generated through a single connected interface, with no local machine involved. Current best options, checked 17 October 2026:

| Model | Why | Rough cost |
|---|---|---|
| Google Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS | Currently ranked first for text-to-speech quality, cheapest of the good ones | about 9 USD per million output tokens |
| Microsoft MAI-Voice-2 | Currently ranked first for sounding like a genuine human recording | about 22 USD per million tokens |
| ElevenLabs Multilingual v2 | Built for long, consistent narration | about 40 USD per million tokens |

A whole Chronicle is a few thousand words, so narration costs **under five dollars** per site.

**Voice cloning, when sold.** Requires a HeyGen account and key. This is the only part of the stack with a per-seat commercial dependency, and it is only engaged when a client has paid for it.

### 9.5 The domain ownership question, unresolved

Two options, and the plan does not yet choose:

**Option A: the business holds the domain and account.** Simpler, makes the care fee natural, and lets everything be fixed without the client. But the client's heritage depends on the business continuing to exist, which directly contradicts the product's own promise of permanence.

**Option B: the client owns the domain in their own account, access delegated.** Honest, matches the promise, and survives the business failing. But setup is harder, every change may need the client, and the care fee is harder to justify.

**Recommendation: Option A during build and the first care year, with a written, tested transfer path and a documented dead-man clause naming who inherits.** A heritage business whose clients' heritage dies with it has an unacceptable contradiction at its centre. This must be resolved before the first contract is signed, and the reviewer's opinion is specifically requested.

### 9.6 Security and data handling

- Client repositories are private. Content includes private family letters and photographs of living people.
- No client secret is ever in a repository, in a chat, or in any committed file. Secrets are typed directly into the hosting provider's secret store by the operator alone.
- Each analytics key is scoped to that one client's domain, with read-only permissions. A leaked key exposes one site's traffic counts and nothing else.
- Each dashboard password protects one client's reader numbers only.
- Voice recordings used for cloning are personal biometric-adjacent data and require their own storage, retention and deletion policy. **This does not yet exist.**

## 10. GO TO MARKET

### 10.1 Channels, in order of expected efficiency

**1. Warm diaspora referral.** The first five clients will come from people who have seen the reference site and know the family. Highest conversion, zero cost, and the only channel available before proof exists.

**2. Occasion intermediaries.** The people who already know a milestone is approaching: private wealth managers, family office advisers, estate planning lawyers, luxury concierge services, and high-end event planners. They are asked for gift ideas for exactly this buyer. Referral commission is appropriate.

**3. Corporate direct.** Company secretaries, heads of communications and chief-of-staff roles at firms approaching a founding anniversary. Found from public records. Approached twelve to eighteen months ahead.

**4. Content.** A small number of essays on why AI-generated family history is a forgery, and what honest family recording requires. Positions the integrity rule as a stance rather than a feature. Slow, but it compounds and it attracts exactly the right buyer.

**Explicitly not pursued:** paid advertising, marketplaces, and anything competing on price. Every one of them drags the product into Market A.

### 10.2 The sales conversation

Three moves.

**Anchor high and elsewhere.** "A commissioned memoir runs from three thousand to over forty thousand dollars, and it is a book that is finished the day it prints."

**Demonstrate the integrity rule physically.** Show the sources file and the marked gaps. Say plainly: anything we cannot verify does not go on the site, and you will see every gap we could not fill. This is what separates us from a chatbot that will invent a lovely story about your father.

**Name the deadline, never manufacture it.** The milestone date is real. The subject's ability to record their own voice is genuinely time-limited. Say it once, calmly, and never say it to a grieving family.

## 12. SWOT ANALYSIS

### STRENGTHS

**A genuinely differentiated product with no visible direct competitor.** Nobody else sells a researched, sourced, narrated, living, family-maintainable archive. The nearest competitors are printed books or fill-in templates.

**The integrity rule is a real moat.** As AI-generated family history floods the market, verifiable non-invention becomes more valuable, not less. It is also very hard to retrofit, because it is a discipline rather than a feature.

**Near-zero marginal infrastructure cost.** Under 25 dollars to deliver, 15 dollars a year to run, against a 5,500 to 9,000 dollar price. Margin is effectively unconstrained.

**A finished, live, emotionally effective reference build.** Not a mockup. It works, it is published, and it makes people cry.

**Real engineering assets already paid for.** The scroll-directed film, the narration-driven auto-scroll, the privacy-preserving analytics, and a build that refuses to publish a house-style error. Market research shows motion design alone is billed as a separate premium line item.

**Recurring revenue built into the product.** The care fee is normal in the local market, trivially cheap to deliver, and compounds with every delivery.

**A permanent emotional driver.** Ageing parents and milestone birthdays are not a trend. The demand is structural and the deadlines are real.

### WEAKNESSES

**No commercial proof whatsoever.** One site, for the author's own father, with no paying client, no testimonial and no portfolio. Every early sale will be harder and cheaper than the plan assumes.

**Nothing is reusable yet.** No template exists. A second site currently means hand-copying files out of a repository full of the author's own family's private letters. Until this is fixed, every build costs full effort and the margin is theoretical.

**The capacity estimate is a guess.** Sixty to one hundred and twenty hours per Chronicle is extrapolated from a single build that included inventing the product. If the true figure is two hundred hours, the base case collapses.

**One person is the entire company.** Sales, research, writing, building, support and custody. Illness, distraction or a day job removes all capacity at once.

**No legal infrastructure.** No contract, no consent forms, no takedown policy, no privacy notice, no professional indemnity insurance, no voice-recording retention policy. The business is currently uninsured against its single largest risk.

**The author is not a developer** and depends on AI assistance for every technical change. Capable with it, blocked without it.

**No published pricing and no sales experience** in a category where the author has never sold anything.

**The home market anchors too low.** Malaysian buyers will compare against RM 8,000 to 20,000 web quotes, so the primary market must be export, which is harder to reach from a standing start.

### OPPORTUNITIES

**The corporate segment is larger, easier and less emotional.** Founder tribute books already sell for 10,000 to 50,000 dollars, companies are commissioning them instead of retirement gifts, and a link beats a book nobody opens. Budget exists, no grief is involved, and one happy company refers others.

**Intermediary channels are untapped.** Wealth managers and estate planners are repeatedly asked for exactly this and have nothing good to recommend.

**Voice cloning is a high-margin, genuinely unique and genuinely urgent upsell**, and no competitor offers it.

**A printed companion volume is an obvious second product**, reaching the buyer who still wants a book, at near-zero incremental research cost.

**Licensing the method** to funeral homes, genealogy firms or private banks is a plausible later path once the template and the process are proven.

**AI-generated competitors will damage trust in the category**, which makes a documented integrity rule more saleable over time rather than less.

**Adjacent markets exist**: institutions, schools, temples, clubs and family businesses all have founders and anniversaries.

### THREATS

**Consent failure is existential.** The product publishes living people's faces, private letters and cloned voices. One family dispute without signed consent can end the venture and damage the author's name permanently. This is the single largest risk in the plan, it is currently unmitigated, and it is not a formality.

**Voice cloning carries specific and evolving legal exposure.** Biometric and likeness law is tightening across jurisdictions, and a cloned voice of a real person is squarely in its path. Written consent is necessary and may not be sufficient everywhere.

**Cheap AI memoir products at 149 to 349 dollars will train buyers to expect low prices** and will degrade trust in anything AI-assisted, including honest work.

**A single platform holds hosting, domain, publishing and analytics.** Excellent while free and highly concentrated. A pricing change, policy change or account suspension affects every client site at once.

**Client dependency on the business contradicts the product's promise.** Unresolved, as set out in Section 9.5.

**A hostile outcome is possible even with perfect delivery.** Families contain conflict. A Chronicle can become the object of an inheritance dispute, and the author can be drawn into it.

**Fact error risk.** A single wrong, published fact about a real and prominent person is reputationally far more expensive than the fee. The research found that multiple people share most names, which is exactly how this happens.

**Long sales cycles tied to milestones** mean revenue arrives lumpy and unpredictably, which is hard to survive without other income.

**The founder is the product.** The craft, taste and judgment are not yet documented or transferable, so the business cannot currently be sold or scaled.

## 13. RISK REGISTER AND MITIGATIONS

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consent dispute over a living person's image, words or voice | **Existential** | Signed per-person consent covering image, words and voice separately, plus a written takedown route honoured first and discussed after | **Not started** |
| Voice cloning legal exposure | High | Living, consenting, self-recorded subjects only. Never the deceased. Written retention and deletion policy. Legal review in each selling jurisdiction | **Not started** |
| Published factual error about a real person | High | Source record for every fact, identity warning per subject, no unsourced sentence ships, family sign-off before publishing | **Working, undocumented as a contract term** |
| Capacity estimate wrong by 2x | High | Measure the next build hour by hour. Do not sign a third client until the second is measured | **Not started** |
| Single-platform concentration | Medium | Repository is a full backup. Document and rehearse a migration path to another host | **Partially mitigated** |
| Client stranded if the business fails | Medium | Written succession and transfer clause in every contract, tested once | **Not started** |
| No insurance | Medium | Obtain professional indemnity cover before the first paid commission | **Not started** |
| Author unavailable | Medium | Document the method. Contract researcher relationship established early | **Partially started** |
| Price anchoring to the local web market | Medium | Export-first positioning, language discipline, never quote against a web brief | **Addressed in this plan** |

**Four items in the above table must be complete before the first money is accepted:** consent pack, contract with succession clause, voice policy, and insurance.
