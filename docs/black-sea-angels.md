# The Thirteen Black Sea Angels

*The last beings made by the Titan of Fear, out in space.*

---

## What that one sentence decides

**Made, not born.** There is no ecology behind these. No species, no young, no
adaptation. Something built them on purpose, and a maker's hand should be
visible in all thirteen: repetition, deliberate proportion, parts that match
because one mind chose them. They should look authored.

**Made *of* fear.** Not decorated with fear, not frightening in the way a
predator is frightening. Fear is the material they are made from, the way a
statue is made of marble. That means they do not need teeth or claws, and
putting any on them weakens them.

**From space.** Not from the deep, not from below, not from hell. Nothing in
their construction should belong to this world's biology, geology or weather.
If a part of one looks like it evolved somewhere, it is wrong.

**The last ones.** This is the richest part and the easiest to waste. The
Titan of Fear made these and then made nothing further. Thirteen is all there
will ever be. They are a final work, and a final work made by something that
was ending.

---

## Why your two attempts read wrong

Hood, glowing eyes, four skeletal arms, bat wings, gold ornament. That is your
world's existing vocabulary for infernal things, and these are not infernal,
not of this world, and not alive in the way demons are alive. Gold is the
worst offender: ornament implies vanity, culture and an audience, and a thing
built out of fear by a dying Titan has none of those.

**And my own first pass was half wrong too.** I built it as a deep-sea colony,
which is good for the swallowing and bad for the origin. Jellyfish and
barnacles are this world's biology. The colony idea survives, because a swarm
holding a shape is genuinely unsettling and it explains the infection. What has
to change is what the swarm is *made of*: not sea life, but identical
manufactured parts that resemble nothing that ever evolved.

## The five laws

**1. A swarm of identical made things, holding a shape.** Thousands of small
parts, every one the same as every other, assembled into a rough form. The
edges blur into individuals still finding their place. Not organs, not limbs,
not flesh. Think components, not creatures.

**2. One light, and it is the halo.** A single cold form of light where a halo
would be, and nothing else glows. It lights nothing around it. This is the only
reason terrified witnesses reached for the word *angel*, and it is the family
resemblance across all thirteen.

**3. Unarmed, always.** No claws, no teeth, no blades, no fighting limb. One
of these beat a god by being swallowed. If it looks like it could win a fight,
the design has failed.

**4. Symmetry that is wrong rather than absent.** Radial, fivefold, or mirrored
on an axis bodies do not use. Never a clean left and right with a front and a
back. The eye should keep trying to find the face and keep failing.

**5. It induces the fear, it does not display it.** None of them looks
frightened and none of them looks fierce. The feeling belongs to the viewer.

**Locked palette:** bone white, black, and one cold colour. No red, no gold,
no warm light, anywhere, ever.

## The thirteen fears

Here is where thirteen distinct designs come from without thirteen arbitrary
inventions. **One fear each.** The Titan made one of each, and then stopped.

falling · drowning · being watched · the dark · being buried ·
being forgotten · open space with no shelter · enclosure · the thing behind
you · losing someone · your own body betraying you · the stranger who knows
your name · the end

The fear decides the shape, and the shape writes itself:

- **Falling** holds the form of something mid-collapse that never lands.
- **Being watched** is the one with no eyes anywhere, and you feel them anyway.
- **Enclosure** holds the shape of a doorway, and it is the wrong size.
- **Being buried** is half sunk into the ground and still descending.
- **The thing behind you** is only ever drawn from behind, and the silhouette
  is almost a person.
- **Being forgotten** is the one whose parts are already drifting away.

That last one should be the smallest.

## The descent of completeness

Take this and the set gains a spine.

They were made as their maker was ending. So **they descend in completeness
from the first to the thirteenth.** The first is a masterwork: exact, finished,
every part seated. By the seventh the assembly is loosening. The thirteenth is
barely held together, parts missing, the shape only approximate, as though the
Titan ran out of whatever it had been spending.

**The thirteenth is the one Bahamut swallowed.**

He destroyed an entire fleet single-handedly and was undone by the least
finished thing in it. Not the leader. Not the most beautiful. The runt, the
offcut, the one nobody would have looked at twice, which is also exactly why
his siblings did not take the warning seriously.

That is a better tragedy than losing to the strongest, and it tells you how to
draw it: modest, small, unfinished, almost disappointing. Put it beside the
other twelve and no one would pick it out.

## The master prompt

Fill the four bracketed slots. Everything else is fixed, and the fixed part is
what makes thirteen pictures look like one family.

> A thing from outside the world, made rather than born, [SETTING]. It is not
> one creature. It is a swarm of thousands of small identical manufactured
> parts, pale and translucent, resembling nothing that ever evolved, holding
> themselves together in the rough shape of [THE FORM]. The shape is
> approximate, the way a swarm is approximate, and the edges blur into
> individual parts still drifting into place.
>
> It was built on purpose by one hand: the parts repeat exactly, and the
> proportions are deliberate. It is [radially / fivefold] symmetrical, never
> bilaterally. No face, no eyes, no mouth. The eye keeps looking for a face and
> keeps failing.
>
> Its one light is [THE HALO FORM], cold [COLOUR], glowing from inside the gaps
> between the parts rather than lighting anything around it. That light is the
> only reason a terrified witness would ever have said the word angel.
>
> It is unarmed. Nothing on it could cut, grip or strike, and that is correct:
> it wins by being taken in.
>
> Wide shot, the form small and centred, no horizon. Three quarters of the
> frame is black with no detail at all. One light source: itself, dim.
>
> Palette: bone white, black, one cold [COLOUR]. No red, no gold, no warm
> colour anywhere.
>
> Scale: [a wreck / a drowned tower / a small boat] nearby for size.
>
> Painted illustration. Manufactured and wrong, never infernal and never
> biological. Patient, beautiful, deeply unwelcome. Large areas carry no detail.
>
> Avoid: hoods, cloaks, robes, glowing eyes, skeletal arms, bat wings,
> feathered wings, horns, skulls, teeth, claws, gold, demons, armour, a
> humanoid face, fire, red, sea creatures, a dramatic pose.

## Three worked examples

**The First — Falling.** The masterwork. Every part exactly seated, the
assembly flawless. Holds the shape of a figure caught mid-collapse, arms above
its head, permanently about to hit something it never reaches. Halo is a single
hard ring, cold violet. Scale: a drowned tower it is falling past and will not
pass.

**The Seventh — The Thing Behind You.** Drawn only from behind, always. The
silhouette is very nearly a person, and the proportions are wrong by a few
percent in a way that is hard to name. The assembly has begun to loosen; a few
parts hang slightly out of true. Halo is a broken arc, pale cyan. Scale: a
small boat ahead of it, with someone in it who has not turned round.

**The Thirteenth — Being Forgotten.** The one he swallowed. Smallest, least
finished, parts visibly missing and more drifting away as you watch, the shape
barely holding. Halo is a single dim low star, colourless white, the plainest
of the thirteen. Scale: a man's boat beside it, and it is not much bigger.
Nothing about it looks dangerous. Nothing about it looks worth a god's
attention. That was the point.

---

## Two questions for you

**Is the Titan of Fear dead, or spent, or still out there?** If these are its
last works because it died, they are a will and testament. If it is merely
spent, the Foundation Age has something still waiting in the dark that can
make more once it recovers. Those are very different stories and they change
how the thirteen should feel.

**Did all thirteen come to the world, or only the fleet Bahamut met?** If some
never arrived, they are still out there, and the Black Sea wars that follow
have an author rather than a cause.
