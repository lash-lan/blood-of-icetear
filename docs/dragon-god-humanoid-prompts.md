# The Five Children of Ra, in the visage they chose

A dragon taking a humanoid shape is not a dragon shrinking. It is a god
choosing a face, borrowing from elves and humans and whatever else it has
looked upon, and keeping a few things of its own because it will not give
them up.

That last part is the whole design problem. Keep too little and you have
drawn an elf. Keep too much and you have drawn a lizard in a robe.

---

## The rule that makes a visage read

**Three retentions. Never more.**

Pick exactly three draconic things and let everything else be a person. One
for the **eyes**, one for a **surface**, one for a **structure**. Three reads
as deliberate. Six reads as a costume.

**The face stays human or elven in its shape.** The draconic shows in
material, never in anatomy. A muzzle makes a beast. A human jaw sheathed in
fine scale makes a god wearing a face.

**Get the proportion wrong by a few percent.** Slightly too tall. Neck a
little too long. Too still, as though breathing is optional. Uncanny is a
small number, not a large one.

**They are taller than any mortal, and that is shown, never stated.**

Height is a comparison. A model cannot draw "tall" because tall is not a
property of a shape, it is a relationship between two shapes. So every one of
these needs a mortal in the frame, and the god measured against them.

Four things do the work together:

- **A mortal in shot**, small, and the comparison made explicit: *a head and
  shoulders taller than the tallest man present*. One person is enough.
- **A low camera**, so the viewer looks up. Put the god's head near the top
  edge and let the feet go out of frame or sit far below.
- **Longer proportion.** A heroic figure is about eight heads tall. Ask for
  **nine**: longer limbs, longer neck, a head slightly small for the body.
  That alone reads as not-human before anything else registers.
- **A door, a step, a window** of known size in the frame, to measure against.

**Striking is shown by other people's behaviour.** This is the part that gets
missed. You do not make a face more beautiful by adding gold to it. You make
it beautiful by showing what it does to the room: the mortal who has stopped
mid-step, or looked away, or will not come closer. Give every one of these a
mortal who is reacting, and the god will read as overwhelming without a single
ornament.

**The armour is not the character.** This is the trap that ruined the first
dragon images, wearing a different costume. The instinct for a god is to pile
on ornate plate, filigree, gold, layered robes. Resist it. Give each of them
*one* significant object and let the rest be plain cloth. A king in a plain
coat reads as a king. A king in covered-in-everything reads as a card game.

**Carry over the craft rules from the beast prompts:** one named light source,
three colours, large areas with no detail, a readable silhouette, and the
banned words list. `masterpiece`, `ultra detailed`, `intricate`, `ornate`,
`8k`, `epic`, `majestic` all push toward uniform density and will undo this.

## Two of these depend on lore you have not settled

I am flagging rather than guessing.

**Bahamut is sealed in enchanted slumber beneath the earth.** So the honest
question is whether he still has a visage at all. Three readings, and they
produce three different pictures:

- The form he wore **before** the Black Sea, as memory or as a portrait
  hanging in a hall. Grief for what was lost.
- The form he wears **now, asleep**, corrupted, which only Tiamatra ever sees.
- The form that **walks in dreams**, because something that swallowed an
  Angel does not necessarily stay where it is put.

The prompt below takes the second, because it is the one that puts Tiamatra's
burden on screen. Say the word and I will write the other two.

**Osiris, Ryujin and Delphyne have not chosen yet.** The Law of Divine
Departure gave them a return to Ra's pocket realms at full power, or staying
at ten percent. That choice decides whether their visage is radiant and whole
or worn thin like Tiamatra's. I have written all three as **departed and
undiminished**, since only Tiamatra is recorded as staying. If any of them
stayed, tell me and I will dim them.

## 1. Osiris — the Phoenix-Dragon

*Rebirth, sacred fire. Father of the feathered dragons and the High Phoenixes.*

**Three retentions:** eyes with no whites, solid ember from lid to lid; fine
down of red feathers at the temples and the backs of the hands instead of
hair and skin; a faint heat shimmer in the air around him at all times.

> A tall man of ambiguous age, elven in the length of the bones and human in
> the warmth of the face, standing in a bare stone hall. His hair is not hair
> but short dark red feathers lying flat, and the same down runs over the
> backs of his hands. His eyes are solid ember with no whites. The air an inch
> from his skin ripples with heat.
>
> He is holding one small blue flame in a cupped palm and looking at it, not
> at the viewer. It is the only blue thing in the picture. He is about to give
> it away, or he has just taken it back.
>
> Nine heads tall, long limbed, the head a little small for the body. A
> mortal servant stands at the edge of the frame holding a bowl, and the top
> of his head reaches only Osiris's shoulder. The servant is not looking at
> him and plainly does not dare.
>
> Low angle from the servant's height, so the viewer looks up. Osiris slightly
> off centre to the left with his head near the top edge, the right half of
> the frame empty stone wall.
>
> One light source: the blue flame in his hand, lighting him from below, so
> the upper half of his face is in soft shadow.
>
> Palette: ash grey stone, dark ember red, one cold blue. Nothing else. The
> wall behind him carries no detail at all. Detail only on the hand and the
> flame.
>
> He wears plain undyed linen, no armour, no jewellery. The feathers are the
> only ornament he needs.
>
> Painted fantasy portrait. Noble, ancient, unhurried.
> Avoid: wings, crowns, armour, gold, fire everywhere, glowing runes.

## 2. Tiamatra — the Dragon Queen

*Sovereignty and legacy. She stayed. She gave up ninety percent of herself to
hold her brother's prison shut, and she lost a favoured daughter.*

**Three retentions:** heavy iron-grey scale across the shoulders and collar
bones where a mantle would sit; a low crest of short bone horns through the
hair; hands that are a little too large, with dark nails like claw sheaths.

> A woman whose face is young and always will be, no older than thirty and
> entirely unlined, tall and broad through the shoulders, elven in the ears.
> She does not age and never has. Her dark hair has gone grey anyway, which
> against that face is the first thing that looks wrong about her. A low crest of short bone horns runs back through it like a circlet
> that grew there. Heavy iron-grey scale lies across her shoulders and collar
> bones where a mantle would sit, and her hands are slightly too large, the
> nails dark and thick.
>
> She is sitting on a plain stone step, not a throne, elbows on her knees,
> looking at the ground between her feet. She has been sitting there a long
> time. This is a watch being kept, not a pose. She is tired in the way that
> sleep does not fix.
>
> Even seated she is enormous. A mortal steward stands beside her holding a
> lamp, and sitting down her eyes are level with his standing. Nine heads tall
> were she to rise. He is watching her and she has not acknowledged him.
>
> Full figure, seated to one side, low camera at the steward's waist so the
> viewer looks up at both. Most of the picture is the bare floor and the dark
> behind her.
>
> One light source: cold daylight from a high window out of frame, falling on
> her shoulders and hands and leaving her face half in shadow.
>
> Palette: iron grey, stone, and one deep red in the worn cloth around her.
> Nothing bright anywhere. At least half the frame is near empty.
>
> She wears a plain heavy coat, travel-worn. No crown, no armour, no gold. One
> object only: a ring on a cord at her throat, too small for her hand, which
> belonged to her daughter.
>
> Painted fantasy portrait. Restrained, heavy, patient, sad.
> Avoid: thrones, crowns, armour, wings, fire, a proud chin-up pose, anything
> triumphant.

## 3. Bahamut — the Black, as he is now

*Strength and judgment. He saw the threat first, acted alone, won, and the
winning was what destroyed him. Asleep beneath the earth.*

**Three retentions:** two mutilated bone stumps at the shoulder blades where
wings were cut short; black veining under the skin that moves slightly of its
own accord; a second light behind the eyes that does not match the first.

> A powerfully built man lying asleep on his back on bare rock, deep
> underground. He was beautiful once and the bones of that are still in the
> face. Elven and human both, dark skinned, jaw slack in sleep.
>
> From his shoulder blades rise two bone stumps, snapped off short, the
> ragged ends healed over badly. Under his skin, black veining spreads from
> the old wounds across his chest and throat, and it is not quite still. His
> eyes are open a fraction, and the light in them is not his. Something else
> is awake in there while he is not.
>
> He is vast. A single small figure in a heavy coat stands on the rock near
> his shoulder, no taller than his upper arm is thick, head bowed. She comes
> here often.
>
> Seen from high above, looking down, his body running diagonally across the
> frame. He takes up less of the picture than the rock around him, and she is
> barely more than a mark on it.
>
> One light source: a thin cold shaft from far above, falling across his chest
> and leaving his face and the stumps in near darkness.
>
> Palette: black, wet grey stone, and one sickly blue in the veining and the
> eyes. Nothing warm. Two thirds of the frame is unlit rock with no detail.
>
> No clothing but the remains of something that was once fine, rotted to
> threads. No chains, no obvious prison. The stillness is the prison.
>
> Painted fantasy portrait. Tragic and quiet, a tomb effigy that is breathing.
> He should look like a hero laid out with honour, if you did not look closely.
> Avoid: monsters, claws, roaring, chains, action, red, fire, spikes.

## 4. Ryujin — the Wise

*Water, wisdom, magic. He flies with no wings because he does not need them.*

**Three retentions:** long trailing whiskers from the upper lip, fine and
white, that drift as though underwater; translucent fins where the ears would
be; pale jade scale along the forearms and the backs of the hands.

> An old man, slender, very upright, standing on the surface of still water
> without breaking it. The face is human and kind and deeply lined, the ears
> replaced by fins of thin translucent membrane. Two long white whiskers trail
> from his upper lip and drift slowly upward, as if he and they were
> underwater and the rest of the world was not. Pale jade scale runs along his
> forearms.
>
> His hair and his robes are also drifting upward, weightless. Nothing else in
> the picture is. He is reading something small held in both hands and has not
> looked up.
>
> He is far taller than a man, nine heads, elongated and narrow. A small
> fishing boat sits on the water nearby with one fisherman in it, kneeling,
> his face turned down and away. Standing, the fisherman would not reach
> Ryujin's chest.
>
> Full figure, centred, low camera near the water, with a great deal of flat
> water around him and flat pale sky above.
>
> One light source: diffuse overcast light from directly overhead, no sun.
>
> Palette: pale jade, white, and the near black of deep still water. Nothing
> saturated. Three quarters of the frame is empty water and sky with no detail
> whatsoever. His reflection is present and perfectly sharp.
>
> Plain unbelted robe, no ornament at all. One object: whatever he is reading.
>
> Painted fantasy portrait in an ink and wash manner. Unhurried, courteous,
> immensely old.
> Avoid: beards like a stereotype sage, pagodas, cherry blossom, glowing orbs,
> particles, sparkles, dragons coiling in the background.

## 5. Delphyne — the Great Winged, Queen of Winds

*Freedom and prophecy. Her brood became the Wyverns, whose forelimbs are
wings. Hers should be too.*

**Three retentions:** arms that are wings, the long feathered membrane running
from wrist to hip, so she has no free hands; eyes with a horizontal pupil like
a bird of prey; hair that behaves as though in a wind that is not blowing.

> A woman standing on a bare clifftop with her back to a vast drop. Her arms
> are not arms. From wrist to hip runs a long feathered membrane, so what
> would be her hands are the leading edge of a wing, folded in close at her
> sides like a cloak she cannot take off. Her face is elven and sharp and
> young, with a horizontal slot of a pupil in a pale eye. Her hair lifts and
> moves in a wind that is not touching the grass.
>
> She is looking slightly past the viewer, at something further off and
> further ahead, and she has already decided not to tell you what it is.
>
> She is a head and shoulders taller than any mortal, nine heads, long in the
> leg and neck. A shepherd has come up the path behind her and stopped, well
> back, and is not going to come any closer. He reaches her shoulder at most.
>
> Three quarter figure, low camera from the shepherd's position looking up,
> placed low and to the right, with the open sky filling the upper two thirds
> of the frame.
>
> One light source: hard high sun from behind her, so she is rim lit along one
> edge and her front is in flat shadow.
>
> Palette: cloud white, canvas grey, deep shadow blue. No warm colour. The sky
> is a single graded wash with nothing in it.
>
> She wears a simple wrapped garment that leaves the wing-arms free. No
> jewellery, no armour. One object: nothing. She carries nothing, because she
> cannot.
>
> Painted fantasy portrait. Remote, certain, slightly unsettling.
> Avoid: a cloak that is actually a cape, normal arms with wings added behind,
> feathers everywhere, storms, lightning, a dramatic sky, anything cluttered.

## The thing that ties the five together

Give all five the same light quality, the same restraint and the same canvas
shape, and they will read as siblings even though their anatomy differs.
Change only the palette:

| | Palette | Feeling |
|---|---|---|
| Osiris | ash, ember red, one blue | life returning |
| Tiamatra | iron, stone, one deep red | duty enduring |
| Bahamut | black, wet grey, sick blue | strength undone |
| Ryujin | jade, white, deep water | wisdom guiding |
| Delphyne | cloud white, grey, shadow blue | freedom prevailing |

Bahamut is the only one with a cold colour where the others have warmth or
calm. That is the point, and it should be visible at a glance when the five
hang side by side.
